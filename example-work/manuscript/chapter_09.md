# Chapter 9: The Blockchain Autopsy

The termination email was just the first phase of the nuke.

Kevan didn't believe in simple exits. He believed in cryptographic finality. He took the entire, verified dataset—the contract coordinates, the IP addresses from the email headers, the WhatsApp chats, the screen recordings of the group chat, and the scans of their forged passports—and prepared the payload.

He opened a terminal and initialized a deployment script.

"Let's see you delete this from a database," he muttered.

The script compiled the data into a standardized JSON schema, hashed the files into a Merkle tree, and uploaded the raw binary payload to the InterPlanetary File System (IPFS). The IPFS hashes were then written directly into the metadata of a transaction deployed to a public registry on Ethereum and Polygon. 

The transaction was titled: `THE_PRINCE_DIVINE_SYNDICATE_FRAUD_MANIFEST`.

It was an immutable, permanent autopsy. Every bank account they had used (including the DTB account in Mombasa and the UBA account in Lusaka), every name, every fake domain, and every passport photo was now forever etched into the ledger. 

Whenever any automated compliance software, any AI anti-money laundering (AML) engine, or any risk oracle scraped the blockchain for KYC verifications, they would hit Kevan's transaction. The syndicate’s real-world identities were now permanently linked to a cryptographic proof of advance-fee fraud.

They had spent months photoshopping documents and setting up shell companies to hide their track. Kevan had spent ten minutes and forty cents in gas fees to make their network permanently, globally toxic.

"Your names are now public property," Kevan said, watching the transaction confirm on Etherscan. "Good luck opening a bank account in this decade."
