# Chapter 2: The Royal Dick Pic

Prince Divine didn't waste time. Within minutes of Kevan's reply, the WhatsApp chat was a firehose of incoming media. 

But these weren't regular files. To a normal investor, this was the holy grail of physical commodity verification. To Kevan, it was a gallery of digital props.

First came the PDFs. 

`Brinks_SKR_Douala_Transit_6000KG.pdf`  
`Al_Sawaya_Gold_Assay_Dubai_Refinery.pdf`  
`Draft_Chase_MT760_SBLC_40M.pdf`

"Look at this beauty, my brother," Prince Divine's voice note crackled through the speaker, thick with a Zambian accent, accompanied by the background roar of what sounded like a busy Lusaka street. "This is clean gold. All certified. The assay labs in Dubai have checked the purity—999.9 fine. We have the Safe-Keeping Receipt from Brinks in Cameroon. And we have a bank-guaranteed Standby Letter of Credit draft for forty million dollars from Chase. We are ready to ship to your vault. We only need a partner with the technology to make it move on the blockchain."

Kevan downloaded the documents to his local staging directory. He opened the Brinks SKR first.

It looked magnificent. It had the classic Brinks logo, a series of complex serial numbers, official-looking stamp marks in blue ink, and the signature of a "Regional Security Director" named Marc. The assay certificate from Al Sawaya Gold Group in Dubai was printed on thick, security-bordered paper, complete with gold foil seals and chemical analysis charts showing the exact metallurgical breakdown of the bars.

Then came the images. 

Prince Divine started sending photos of the actual bullion. Low-res, slightly blurry shots of wooden crates packed with shiny yellow bars, each stamped with the word `INTEGROUS`. In one picture, a hand holding a copy of that day's newspaper was positioned next to the gold to prove the photo was fresh.

Even Kevan's compliance guy, when pinged on Slack with the files, sent back a cautious reply:  
`"Bro... I'm looking at these seals. Usually, these guys send Microsoft Word docs with clip art. These assays look surprisingly clean. The SWIFT MT760 draft has actual routing coordinates. Are we actually about to buy a gold mine?"`

Kevan leaned into his monitor, zooming in on the pixel grid of the signatures. 

"They're good," Kevan murmured. "The stamps have natural ink bleed. The paper texture on the scans is consistent. If I were a normal VC looking for a quick tokenization play, I'd already be signing the NDA and wiring the deposits."

He opened a terminal window, initialized his forensic tools, and ran a metadata extraction script on the PDF files. 

"Let's see who actually printed the paper."
