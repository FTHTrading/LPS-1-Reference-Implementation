# Chapter 6: International Circle Jerk

The moment Kevan pushed back on the Mumbwa mining claim, the syndicate scrambled. 

They didn't back down. Instead, they spun up the international machinery to manufacture a thicker layer of credibility. It was like watching a global circle jerk where every actor was desperately trying to keep the momentum going.

First, a Vietnamese company named Viet-Zam Group JSC emerged from the shadows. 

An email from `nguyen.cong.ninh@vietzam.vn` landed in Kevan’s inbox. The sender claimed to be the Chairman and CFO of a major agricultural and industrial conglomerate based in Ho Chi Minh City. He attached a formal letter of recommendation, stamped with a massive red circular seal, certifying that Viet-Zam had been purchasing gold from Nkamwa Divine Generals for over a decade.

"We vouch for the integrity of Prince Divine," the translation read. "Our group has processed millions of dollars in commodities with his family. The gold in Douala is genuine."

Kevan pulled the Vietnamese tax registries. Viet-Zam Group JSC had been registered exactly three months prior. Its website was a freshly deployed WordPress template, complete with default "Lorem Ipsum" text still visible in the footer. 

But the real smoking gun was hidden in the website's DNS history. Kevan ran a reverse lookup on the registrar data and found the primary recovery email for the Vietnamese domain: `christophermwape5577@gmail.com`.

The "Vietnamese Chairman" was using the exact same free Gmail address as the "Zambian Prince."

At the same time, the Cameroonian side of the network activated. They sent a copy of a BICEC bank clearance certificate under the name of Emerald Africa Ltd. 

Kevan investigated Emerald Africa. They were a real company—a legitimate smart-water metering contractor working on municipal utility pilots in Yaoundé. The scammers had simply hijacked their corporate name, copied their bank details from a public government procurement portal, and set up a lookalike Gmail address to communicate with Unykorn.

It was a beautiful, chaotic web of jurisdictional arbitrage. If you tried to sue them, you had to deal with Zambian dirt plots, Kenyan residential apartments, Vietnamese shell entities, and Cameroonian identity theft. 

They thought they had built an unresolvable maze. Kevan just saw a single, poorly coordinated script.
