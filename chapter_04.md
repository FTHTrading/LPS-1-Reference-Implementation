# Chapter 4: The First Lube Application

Kevan wired the forty-seven thousand dollars. 

He didn't do it because he was fooled. He did it because he wanted to buy a front-row seat to the show. In the grand scheme of his development budget, forty-seven grand was the price of a mid-level research spike. If he was going to write a book exposing cross-border commodity fraud, he needed the actual transaction logs. He needed to apply the first bit of lube to the scam engine to watch how it operated when it thought it had a live fish on the line.

The moment the wire cleared, the dynamic changed.

Prince Divine’s WhatsApp went dark. For four days, the green "online" dot disappeared. No voice notes, no PDFs, no shiny pictures of crates.

On the fifth day, the phone rang. It wasn't Divine. It was a new number with a Kenyan country code.

"Mr. Burns," a smooth, professional voice purred. "My name is John Andrew Mwita. I am the coordinating director for Genesis Mark Holdings in Mombasa. We have been instructed by Prince Divine to facilitate the banking rails for the shipment. There has been a minor complication at the port. The Cameroonian customs authorities have flagged the assay certificates as non-compliant with the new regional export codes."

Kevan smiled, leaning back in his chair. "A complication. Imagine my shock, John."

"Yes, it is very unfortunate," John replied, completely missing the sarcasm. "The local director is asking for a regulatory correction fee. Only twenty-eight thousand dollars. Once this is paid, the Chase MT760 will be activated, and the credit-line facilities will be unlocked. I have the new payment coordinates for Diamond Trust Bank in Mombasa."

Kevan pulled up John's profile. The caller was operating from a residential apartment complex in Mombasa. Behind his professional tone, Kevan could hear the faint sound of a cheap electric fan and chickens clucking in a courtyard.

"Twenty-eight thousand," Kevan said, his voice dripping with mock concern. "Oh no, my compliance guy is going to kill me. Let me see what I can do."

He hung up, opened his terminal, and began running a recursive trace on the email domains the Kenyan had used to send the new "regulatory" paperwork. 

The circle jerk was officially expanding across borders.
