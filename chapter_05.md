# Chapter 5: The 6.6 Hectare Empire

The PACRA registry records for Nkamwa Divine & General Dealers finally landed in Kevan's staging directory. 

He didn't just find a corporate registry; he found a comedy.

Christopher Mwape Nkausu—the self-styled "Prince Divine" whose passport claimed he was born in Chawama, a high-density, low-income township in Lusaka—was listed as the sole shareholder. The company’s registered business scope was not "precious metals mining," "refinery operations," or "international commodity logistics." It was registered for "general wholesale and retail dealer of domestic goods." 

Prince Divine was licensed to sell soap, toilet paper, and matches.

Kevan next pulled the Zambian Ministry of Mines licensing database. He mapped the exact coordinates of the "royal mining claim" Divine had boasted about on WhatsApp. 

He zoomed in on the satellite view. 

The "empire" was a tiny, 6.5981-hectare patch of red dirt in Mumbwa, surrounded by dry scrubland and small-scale artisanal corn plots. It was smaller than Kevan's backyard in California. There were no excavators, no security fences, no processing plants, and certainly no six thousand kilograms of 999.9 gold bullion. There was only a single, manually dug trench and a pile of gravel next to a plastic tarp.

Kevan burst out laughing, the sound echoing through the empty office. 

"Six tonnes of gold," he cackled, screenshotting the satellite image and saving it as `divine_backyard_empire.png`. "The man is running a four hundred million dollar global gold syndicate from a dirt patch the size of a Walmart parking lot."

He opened WhatsApp and messaged Divine: 

`"Hey Prince, looking at the Mumbwa plot coordinates. Looks beautiful. How many miners do you have working the shovels today?"`
