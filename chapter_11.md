# Chapter 11: The Disengagement

The final step was the cleanup. 

Kevan opened Unykorn's internal AML risk engine. The cockpit interface was clean, a dark-themed canvas showing the real-time health of the sovereign AI browser infrastructure. He navigated to the compliance module and initialized a new entry in the local blacklist database.

He pasted the hashes of the syndicate's details:

`IP Address Clusters: 41.223.x.x (Lusaka) / 197.248.x.x (Mombasa) / 195.24.x.x (Douala)`  
`Gmail Relays: christophermwape5577@gmail.com / nkamwadivinegenerals@gmail.com`  
`Corporate Fronts: Genesis Mark Holdings / Viet-Zam Group JSC / Emerald Africa Ltd (Hijacked)`  
`Destination Wallets: 0x455e53CBB86018Ac2B8092FdCd39d8444aFFC3F6 (flagged for association)`

"Block and alert," Kevan commanded, his fingers typing the configuration flags.

From this point forward, if any wallet, domain, or server associated with these coordinates attempted to connect to the Unykorn namespace registry, the system would automatically drop the connection. They wouldn't even get a diagnostic error code. They would just hit a brick wall of silence.

His compliance guy walked into the office, carrying two mugs of fresh coffee. He placed one on Kevan's desk and looked at the blacklisted entries on the monitor.

"So the gold is officially a ghost?" he asked.

"Always was," Kevan said, taking a sip of the hot coffee. "But now the ghost is permanently locked out of our system. The stack is clean."

"What about the forty-seven grand?"

Kevan smiled. "Consider it the tuition fee for the best research project we've run this quarter. We've got the documentation, we've got the blockchain templates, and we've got a complete blueprint of the East African advance-fee corridor. That's worth way more than forty-seven grand in security architecture."

He closed the compliance window. The disengagement was complete.
